## What ran

Three widths (32/64/96), three encoder seeds each, fixed 2x2 mask and 2,223
steps/batch four. The three width-32 checkpoints were reused at exactly the
same budget; six wider models were trained locally from scratch. Every width
used the same actual image order within a seed. Each model saw 8,892 draws,
with repetition, from the 45,000-row training partition.

width and compute. Equal update counts do not make these equal-compute runs.

The 500 fitted and 200 tuning examples are identical across arms. Every arm
receives the same AdamW grid/200-step budget and classifier seeds 29/31/37,
ridge penalties, and neighbor counts 1/5/15. Only tuning accuracy selects k;
its normalization and index remain fixed on confirmation. Votes are uniform,
not calibrated probabilities. Pixel heads are shared within a width comparison;
replicated pixel states across encoder seeds are verified and deduplicated in
aggregate metrics.

AdamW means average classifier seeds within each encoder, then the three
encoder means. Learned 32/64/96 encoder-mean SDs are 1.85/0.70/0.58 percentage
points on shared data, not confidence intervals or independent data uncertainty.

Wider is not consistently better here. The learned 32-D AdamW result exceeds
its random/pixel controls on these fresh rows, while ridge/kNN still favor
pixels. This mixed pilot does not establish a general learned-representation
advantage or a best width. The 32-D weights are unchanged from October 14;
different confirmation examples explain why these percentages must not be
read as a day-over-day model improvement. Random controls now match each
training initialization seed, unlike the earlier 29/31/37 controls.
